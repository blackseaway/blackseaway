The Black Sea has become a war zone, and a trade route at the same time. Grain, steel, fuel and containers still move between Ukraine, Romania, Bulgaria, Türkiye and Georgia. But every voyage now carries risks that did not exist a few years ago: drones and missiles hitting ports, mines drifting into shipping lanes, insurance premiums that change with every attack.

These stories are usually reported one by one: a strike on a port here, a damaged vessel there. What gets lost is the bigger picture. **How often does it happen? Where? And what does it do to the cost of moving goods?**

## What you will find here

- **News from three languages in one feed.** Every three hours we collect reports from international, Romanian and Bulgarian media, tag them by topic and place, and translate the headlines. Each one links to the original source.
- **Data you can check.** Key figures with a date and a source next to each of them, plus charts and a map built automatically from the news.
- **Analysis.** Explainers on war-risk insurance, the maritime corridor, port capacity on the Danube and the Black Sea coast, and what changes for logistics companies.

## Who it is for

We write for people who need to understand this region quickly and accurately: **logistics professionals** planning routes and budgets, **journalists** covering the war and the economy, and **policymakers** in Bucharest, Sofia, Brussels and beyond.
