For most of the war the Black Sea's NATO coastal states watched the fighting from the edge. That changed this autumn, when drones began hitting merchant ships inside the exclusive economic zones of Romania and Bulgaria and, in some cases, inside their territorial waters. Below is a summary of the main decisions and statements of recent weeks.

## Türkiye: a new safe-passage deal

Türkiye, which controls access to the Black Sea under the Montreux Convention, has been the most active mediator. On 17 September Ankara proposed a deal to Russia and Ukraine to stop attacks on civilian shipping, explicitly modelled on the 2022 grain corridor. A Turkish diplomat said the plan could use the territorial waters of coastal states such as Türkiye and Georgia, if they agree. At least 25 Turkish-owned ships had been hit since June, according to AFP. At the end of October President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan again urged both sides to stop the attacks.

## Romania, Bulgaria and Türkiye: closer military cooperation

On 28 October the defence ministers and chiefs of staff of the three countries met in Sofia and jointly condemned the drone attacks on commercial ships. On the same day three sea drones struck the container ship Contship Jet off the Bulgarian coast. Its crew was evacuated to Varna.

The three countries already run a joint mine-countermeasures task group (MCM Black Sea), set up in January 2024. They have agreed to widen its mission from clearing mines to also protecting critical infrastructure such as energy installations, telecom cables and pipelines. This matters especially for Romania, whose offshore Neptun Deep gas project is due to make it the EU's largest gas producer.

## Bulgaria and Romania: asking allies for help

After drones struck two more ships off its coast in October, Bulgaria asked NATO and the EU for more support with maritime surveillance. In mid-November Romanian Prime Minister Luca Niculescu said drones had struck three commercial ships near the Sulina canal and that fighter jets had been scrambled twice. France temporarily moved an Atlantique 2 maritime patrol aircraft to Romania at Bucharest's request.

## NATO: more eyes on the sea

On 20 October NATO announced it was sending additional aircraft, drones and radar systems to the Black Sea after the string of attacks near Romania and Bulgaria. The Alliance's options are limited, however: the Montreux Convention restricts how long warships of non-coastal states can stay in the Black Sea.

## The EU: money for a maritime security hub

During visits to Bucharest and Sofia on 9 and 10 November, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced €50 million and a fixed timeline for the Black Sea Maritime Security Hub. The hub will have two offices, in Constanța and Varna, and is meant to give the region real-time monitoring, information sharing and early warning. Romania and Bulgaria will also receive an extra €10 million for search and rescue, and the EU is providing Copernicus satellite imagery for maritime surveillance.

## What is still missing

None of these steps stops the attacks themselves. A safe-passage agreement needs the consent of both Russia and Ukraine, and so far there is none. The security hub will take time to build. Until then, shipping companies, insurers and crews in the western Black Sea work under the same conditions as before: a war-risk listing covering almost the whole sea, and attacks that now reach the coasts of EU member states.
