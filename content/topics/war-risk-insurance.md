For shipowners and traders, the war in the Black Sea is first of all a question of insurance. Without war-risk cover, banks will not finance cargo, charterers will not fix ships and ports will not accept them.

## The whole sea is now a listed area

On 16 September 2026 the Joint War Committee, which advises the London marine insurance market, extended the Black Sea listed area to the entire basin. Only the territorial waters of Türkiye, Romania, Bulgaria and Georgia remain outside it. The decision followed a sharp rise in strikes on merchant ships far from the coast.

The excluded coastal waters are not free of risk: ships have been hit off Bulgaria, near the Sulina canal and north of the Bosphorus. Policy wording should therefore be checked for those waters too.

## Cover and compensation schemes

- **Unity facility:** state-backed war cover for ships using the Ukrainian corridor, up to US$50 million per ship.
- **Ukrainian state compensation:** since 19 October 2026, up to 80% of assessed damage to civilian ships hit by Russian strikes, when insurers do not pay.
- **Private war-risk market:** cover is available but priced voyage by voyage; reinsurers have been restricting capacity for the Black Sea and the Danube.

## What to do before a voyage

Talk to your broker and war-risk underwriter before fixing, check notification requirements, and follow the security guidance of the NATO Shipping Centre and the coastal states. See our [Data & map](../stats.html) page for current guidance and emergency contacts.
