Ukraine is one of the world's largest exporters of grain and vegetable oils, and most of it leaves by sea. Since Russia left the Black Sea Grain Initiative in July 2023, Ukraine has kept exports going through its own maritime corridor.

## How the corridor works

Ships sail from the ports of Greater Odesa along the western Black Sea, close to the coasts of Romania and Bulgaria, towards the Bosphorus. A second route runs along the Danube from Izmail and Reni through Romanian waters. Both routes depend on mine clearance, naval protection and the willingness of insurers to cover the voyage.

## Pressure on exports

Russian strikes regularly hit port infrastructure, grain terminals and ships in Odesa and on the Danube. At the same time attacks across the open sea have raised war-risk premiums for every voyage. Türkiye has proposed a new safe-passage deal, and Kyiv has offered a ceasefire at sea to protect grain exports, while Moscow has so far rejected the proposals.
