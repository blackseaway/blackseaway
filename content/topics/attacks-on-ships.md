The war between Russia and Ukraine has turned the Black Sea into one of the most dangerous shipping areas in the world. What began as strikes on Ukrainian ports has become a campaign against merchant shipping across the whole basin.

## How the attacks have changed

In the twelve months to 21 September 2026, 45 merchant ships were struck outside the area that insurers previously listed as high-risk, while in the year before there were none. Strikes inside the old risk area rose from an average of 4 to 28 a month. Port facilities were hit 139 times in one quarter, up from 21 in the previous one.

Russian forces continue to strike Odesa, Chornomorsk, Pivdennyi and the Danube ports of Izmail and Reni, and ships calling there. Ukraine, for its part, targets tankers and cargo ships linked to Russian exports, often far offshore: the median distance of these strikes from the coast is about 58 nautical miles.

## Why it matters

Each attack raises the price of moving cargo across the Black Sea. In September the London insurance market declared the whole sea a war-risk area, so every voyage now needs prior notice to insurers and usually an extra premium. Attacks near Romania and Bulgaria have also drawn in NATO and the EU.
